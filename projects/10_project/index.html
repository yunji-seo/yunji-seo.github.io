Han River Evergreen City: Reimagining Seobinggo as a Green Landmark

Fall 2023

This project was completed as part of “Residential Areas Planning and Studio” course at Yonsei University.

View the Final Panel

Overview

“Han River Evergreen City” is a redevelopment proposal for the Seobinggo district in Yongsan, Seoul. The project aims to transform an aging, isolated apartment complex into a vibrant “New City of Yongsan” that connects the urban fabric with nature. By integrating high-rise landmarks with a continuous green axis, the design enhances accessibility to the Han River while solving chronic parking and amenity shortages.

Site Analysis & Issues

The site is located between Yongsan Park (North) and the Han River (South), adjacent to Seobinggo Station (Gyeongui-Jungang Line).

Key Findings:

  • Strengths: Excellent potential for river views and proximity to major green zones. High public transit accessibility via the subway station.
  • Weaknesses:
    • Isolation: The site is physically cut off by highways and underpasses, making pedestrian access difficult.
    • Lack of Amenities: Severe shortage of commercial facilities and schools within walking distance.
    • Aging Infrastructure: Chronic parking shortages and noise from the main road.

Design Concept

The core concept is to create a “Landmark of Seobinggo” that functions as a gateway between the city and nature.

[Fig. 1] 1:2000 Scale Model of the plan

1. Landmark Skyline & Massing

  • Oval Tower Design: To reduce wind load and visual impact, the towers feature rounded, oval forms rather than rigid boxes.
  • Dynamic Height: The plan proposes a mix of building heights ranging from 30 to 50 stories (approx. 175m). The tallest landmark tower (50F) is placed centrally, with heights gradually decreasing towards the river to preserve views.
  • Zigzag Arrangement: Buildings are arranged in a zigzag pattern to ensure unobstructed Han River views for maximum units.

2. Green Axis & Connectivity

  • Curved Green Corridor: A central green axis flows naturally through the site, mimicking the original topography. This creates a pedestrian-friendly “forest path” connecting Seobinggo Station directly to the Han River Park.
  • Extended Greenery: By placing parking and roadways underground, the ground level is dedicated entirely to parks and walking trails, effectively making the Han River park an extension of the residents’ front yard.

3. TOD & Zoning Strategy

  • Transit-Oriented Development (TOD): Commercial facilities are concentrated near the station entrance and main gate to maximize convenience and attract external foot traffic.
  • “Unmix” Community Zoning: To ensure privacy, residential community facilities are separated from the bustling commercial zone. They are placed towards the river/park side, offering quiet spaces with scenic views.

Conclusion

This proposal redefines the Seobinggo apartment complex not just as a residence, but as a “Green City” that bridges the gap between urban transit and natural landscapes. By resolving isolation through strategic green connections and offering a diverse skyline, “Han River Evergreen City” sets a new standard for waterfront redevelopment in Seoul.
