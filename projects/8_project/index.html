Tactical Urbanism: Revitalizing Underused Campus Spaces

Fall 2023

This project was completed as part of “The Theory of Urban Design” course at Yonsei University.

Overview

The goal of this project was to identify a “dead space” (under 330m²) within the campus and transform it into a vibrant social hub. We selected the South Plaza of Engineering Building 3, a 99-pyeong concrete area that was functionally desolate despite its prime location. Through direct observation and behavioral analysis, we proposed a low-cost, high-impact design intervention to turn a mere “passageway” into a “destination.”

Site Analysis: The “Passageway” Problem

The site is located at a key node connecting the Main Gate, Engineering Halls, and the Sports Field. However, it remained empty and bleak.

[Fig. 1] Site Analysis: (Left) The site is a 327m² concrete plaza with a slope. (Right) Analysis showing the space is used strictly for transit (SW↔N) due to lack of amenities.

1. Direct Observation & Data Collection We conducted tracking observations over three days to analyze pedestrian behavior.

[Fig. 2] Data Collection Process: (Left) Detailed observation logs recording behavior and demographics. (Middle & Right) Visualized traffic flow analysis showing peak usage patterns.
  • High Traffic, Zero Stay: While traffic volume was high (up to 445 people/hour during peak times), almost no one stopped.
  • Reasons for Avoidance:
    • Lack of seating or resting facilities.
    • Harsh glare from the sun due to the lack of shade.
    • An artificial, desolate atmosphere created by the tile flooring.

2. Potential Despite its flaws, the site had flat terrain suitable for accessibility and was surrounded by food and coffee spots, indicating a high potential demand for eating and resting areas.

Design Concept: ‘O-S-D’ Plaza

[Fig. 3] The transformation of the plaza using symbolic furniture and landscaping

The design concept ‘O-S-D’ (ㅇㅅㄷ) is derived from the initial Korean consonants of Yonsei University (연세대). We utilized these shapes to create a strong visual identity and functional furniture.

1. Symbolic Furniture (‘ㅇㅅㄷ’ Benches)

[Fig. 4] Custom-designed benches shaped like the university's initials
  • Shape & Function: The massive benches serve as a landmark. The curves of ‘ㅇ’ and ‘S’ create varied seating arrangements, encouraging social interaction (facing inward) or individual rest (facing outward).
  • Integrated Landscaping: Trees are planted within the bench structures to provide natural shade and break the monotony of the concrete floor.

2. Micro-Interventions for Utility

[Fig. 3] H2O Water Fountain and Wall-mounted seats
  • H2O Water Fountain: A molecular-shaped drinking fountain symbolizes the College of Engineering, serving students coming from the sports field.
  • Wall Benches: Space-efficient 1-2 person seats are attached to the retaining walls, featuring department icons to foster a sense of belonging.
  • Sensory Experience: We proposed installing speakers on streetlamps to play music during break times (10 minutes before the hour), changing the auditory landscape of the plaza.

Conclusion: From Path to Place

This proposal aims to shift the space’s function from “Transit” to “Stay.”

By introducing iconic furniture, greenery, and amenities like tables and water fountains, the barren square is reimagined as the “O-S-D Plaza”—a healing spot for engineering students, a picnic area for visitors, and a new landmark for the campus. This project demonstrates how small-scale tactical interventions can significantly improve the quality of campus life.
