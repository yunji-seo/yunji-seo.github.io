This project was completed as part of “Environmental Planning” course at Yonsei University.

Overview

This project examined how mine tailings—residual mineral waste left after ore processing—cause long-term environmental pollution in abandoned metal mines across Korea. Focusing on Korean cases, our team analyzed the generation of acid mine drainage (AMD), heavy-metal contamination pathways, human-health impacts, and the national system responsible for mine reclamation. Building on this analysis, we proposed improved mitigation and restoration strategies tailored to the Korean context.

Background

Mine Tailings as a Source of Pollution

Tailings contain sulfide minerals and heavy metals. When exposed to oxygen or water after mining stops, they oxidize and form acid mine drainage—a highly acidic solution that dissolves metals such as cadmium, arsenic and lead.

According to the report, this process leads to:

  • formation of acidic leachate,
  • mobilization of heavy-metal ions,
  • contamination of groundwater and downstream rivers,
  • long-term ecological and human-health risks.

Human-Health Impacts

The project categorized toxic effects into:

  • Cadmium: kidney dysfunction, bone disease, carcinogenicity.
  • Arsenic: skin lesions, vascular disorders, neurological impacts.
  • Lead: developmental disorders, cardiovascular impacts.

These risks increase when contaminated water or soil accumulates over long periods near residential areas.

Process

1. Review of Korea’s Abandoned Mine Reclamation Framework

The Korean system operates through a sequence of planning stages:

  • Mine Status Survey
  • 5-Year Basic Mine Reclamation Plan
  • Annual Implementation Plan

The national target is to complete restoration for 30% of abandoned mines by 2026. Despite this, actual progress is slow due to limited budgets and institutional bottlenecks.

2. Analysis of 2023 Korea Mine Reclamation Plan

We reviewed the 2023 annual plan to understand:

  • funding structures,
  • project types,
  • remediation priorities,
  • site selection and management.

The plan highlights that AMD treatment, contaminated soil removal, and stabilization works remain the core components of Korean mine restoration.

3. Comparison with U.S. Case: Pennsylvania

To evaluate institutional gaps, we compared Korea with Pennsylvania, one of the most active states in abandoned mine reclamation.

Key differences include:

  • Pennsylvania: multiple actors (volunteer groups, local governments, associations) collaborate to address tailings and AMD.
  • Korea: KOMIR is the single major actor, creating limited flexibility and slower response times.

Analysis

Environmental Pathways of Tailings Pollution

The PPT identifies three main pathways:

  1. Sulfide oxidation → acidic water generation
  2. Heavy-metal dissolution and groundwater contamination
  3. Downstream pollution through runoff and seepage

Limitations of Korean Remediation Policy

We found systemic issues that reduce effectiveness:

  1. Delayed Remediation After Mine Closure

Pollution typically begins immediately after mine abandonment, yet the official remediation process starts later.

  1. Weak Accountability

When mine owners dissolve or disappear, there is no responsible entity to fund cleanup—leaving the cost to government budgets.

  1. Fragmented Restoration Procedures

The process differs between operating mines and abandoned mines, causing bureaucratic delays.

  1. Insufficient Funding and Overreliance on Public Sector

Unlike the U.S., where diverse actors support reclamation financially and operationally, Korea’s reliance on a single agency limits capacity.

Proposed Improvement Strategies

1. Technical Approaches for Mitigating Tailings Pollution

Mitigation Techniques

  • Neutralizing acidic drainage through ADA tests and geochemical evaluation
  • Removing or stabilizing tailings containing heavy metals
  • Using passive limestone drains for continuous treatment

Adaptation Techniques

  • Soil washing and flushing where metal concentrations are high
  • Physically separating contaminated layers
  • Vegetation-based stabilization

2. Strengthening the Institutional System

Based on the report’s recommendations:

  • Require mandatory submission of “post-closure impact plans” during mine shutdown procedures
  • Create annual reclamation funds financed partly by mining operators
  • Increase investment in field monitoring and safety management
  • Diversify stakeholders beyond KOMIR, adopting a U.S.-style collaborative model

Results

Final Output

  • A structured analysis of mine tailings–induced contamination in Korea
  • A review of national and international restoration frameworks
  • A technical comparison of remediation methods
  • Policy proposals tailored to Korea’s environmental and institutional context

Significance

This project demonstrates how environmental planning can integrate geochemical processes, health risk assessment and policy analysis to address long-standing contamination issues caused by mine tailings. It highlights the urgency of proactive remediation in Korea and the need for a more resilient and collaborative national framework.
