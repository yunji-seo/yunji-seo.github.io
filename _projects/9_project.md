_This project was completed as part of "Residential Areas Planning and Studio" course at Yonsei University._

### __Overview__

This project explores the architectural evolution of high-density housing by comparing two distinct paradigms: the **Shinonome Canal Court CODAN** in Tokyo (an open, urban-integrated model) and the **Olympic Athletes' Village Apartments** in Seoul (an isolated, self-contained model). Through 1:2000 scale modeling and hand-drawn plan analysis, our team investigated how physical layout influences social interaction and urban connectivity.
<br>

### __Background & Objective__

[cite_start]As cities densify, the "warmth" of community and "vitality" of urban life are often lost in monolithic housing blocks[cite: 57, 60]. This study aims to identify design strategies that foster interaction.

We selected two case studies to contrast:
1.  [cite_start]**Shinonome CODAN:** A model of urban regeneration that overcomes the defects of former industrial sites to create a "downtown living" environment[cite: 8, 25].
2.  [cite_start]**Olympic Athletes' Village:** A monumental complex that, while innovative for its time, illustrates the issues of urban isolation and internal disconnection[cite: 116, 117].
<br>

__2. The S-Shaped Avenue__
[cite_start]The most defining feature is the **S-shaped pedestrian path** winding through the six blocks[cite: 39].
- [cite_start]**Public Accessibility:** Unlike gated communities, this path allows non-residents to freely access commercial and common facilities located on the ground floor[cite: 40, 41].
- [cite_start]**Human Scale:** Despite a high FAR of 400%, the S-road and low-rise decks mitigate the overwhelming scale of the 14-story towers, creating a comfortable walking environment[cite: 43, 44].

__3. Unit Variety & Transparency__

[cite_start]Designed by six different architect teams, the complex offers 250 unit variations, including SOHO types[cite: 33, 36]. [cite_start]The design uses transparent and semi-transparent materials in common areas (like entryways) to encourage visual communication between neighbors, balancing privacy with community[cite: 53, 54].

__1. Site Context & Design__
[cite_start]Built for the 1988 Olympics, this complex features a unique fan-shaped (radial) layout inspired by the flow of the Seongnae and Gami streams[cite: 102, 105]. [cite_start]It attempted to break the mold of south-facing orientation to prioritize views and skyline variety (6 to 24 stories)[cite: 108, 111].

__2. Critical Analysis: The "Island" Effect__
Despite its architectural significance, our analysis highlighted severe urban disconnection:
- **External Isolation:** Located near the Greenbelt, the complex is surrounded by walls for privacy. [cite_start]This prevents neighbors from accessing natural amenities and forces pedestrians to take long detours, effectively creating a "locked" island[cite: 118, 121].
- [cite_start]**Internal Disconnection:** The radial shape results in vast distances between blocks (up to 1km), discouraging interaction[cite: 125, 126].
- [cite_start]**Loss of Central Space:** The "central courtyard" concept was compromised by surface parking, reducing the green space intended for community gathering[cite: 129, 131].
<br>

### __Results & Conclusion__

<div class="caption">
    "How should high-density housing interact with the city?"
</div>

Through this comparative study and model making, we concluded that **Shinonome CODAN** offers a viable solution for future urban housing.

- [cite_start]**Openness vs. Closure:** While the Olympic Village prioritized exclusivity through walls, Shinonome achieved security and community through "eyes on the street" and mixed-use active frontages[cite: 41, 118].
- [cite_start]**Spatial Configuration:** Shinonome’s S-road proves that high-density complexes can maintain a human scale and vitality if public and private spaces are carefully layered[cite: 43, 59].

The project demonstrates that density does not have to mean "bleakness." [cite_start]By designing for external connection and internal variety, urban housing can be a place of warmth and social exchange[cite: 57, 60].
