### __Overview__

As Korea approaches a super-aged society (projected 20.6% elderly population by 2025), the mobility rights of the elderly have become a critical social issue. This project examines the limitations of current transportation systems for the elderly—specifically safety concerns and the digital divide—and evaluates the applicability of shared mobility solutions (Ride-sharing and Car-sharing). The final proposal suggests a hybrid, community-driven service model tailored to suburban elderly populations.
<br>

### __Problem Identification__

The report identifies three major barriers to elderly mobility:

__1. Safety Issues & License Surrender__

* Physical aging leads to higher accident rates among elderly drivers.
* While voluntary license surrender campaigns exist, they severely restrict mobility in areas with poor public transit, necessitating alternative transport modes.

__2. The Digital Divide__
* **Statistic:** In 2020, 44.6% of the elderly did not own a smartphone. Among those aged 85+, only 0.7% could perform complex tasks like financial transactions via apps.
* **Impact:** Most modern mobility solutions (Uber, Tada, etc.) are app-based, effectively excluding the elderly who lack digital literacy.

__3. Limited Activity Range__
* Elderly mobility is often confined to walking distance or subway lines.
* Approximately 49% of the country consists of areas vulnerable to public transit access, leaving many seniors stranded without a personal vehicle.
<br>

### __Solution Analysis__

We analyzed the pros and cons of existing shared mobility models for the elderly demographic.

| Model | Analysis for Elderly Users |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ride-sharing** | **Pros:** Suitable for non-drivers. "Acquaintance-based" sharing builds trust.<br>**Cons:** Commercial services focus on high-density urban areas. |
| **Car-sharing** | **Pros:** P2P sharing (using neighbors' idle cars) can be cost-effective.<br>**Cons:** Requires the user (or a volunteer) to drive. Accessibility to rental stations is often poor in suburbs. |

<br>

### __Final Proposal: Community-Based Hybrid Mobility__

To overcome the limitations of existing services, I proposed a comprehensive solution:

__1. Community P2P DRT (Demand-Responsive Transport)__
* **Concept:** Utilizing private vehicles owned by local residents to provide paid ride-sharing services. This ensures a sustainable supply of vehicles/drivers within the community compared to volunteer-based models.
* **Operation:** A hybrid system allowing "Analog Calling" (phone/physical hailing) alongside simplified apps to bridge the digital divide.

__2. Safety-First Vehicle Fleet__
* Adopting **Low-Speed Electric Mobility** (max 60km/h) for community sharing vehicles.
* This reduces accident risks for elderly volunteer drivers and is suitable for short-distance travel within suburban neighborhoods.

__3. Financial Support & Vouchers__
* Benchmarking Seattle's **"Ride Now"** program, the proposal includes a voucher system that subsidizes rides for the elderly.
* Offering transfer discounts when connecting to public transit to encourage multi-modal travel.

### __Conclusion__

Mobility is not just a convenience but a fundamental right that dictates one's scope of life. This report concludes that for a super-aged society, technology must be adapted to the user. By combining low-tech interfaces with high-tech dispatch systems and leveraging community resources, we can guarantee safer and more accessible mobility for the elderly.
