_This project was completed as part of the "Environmental Planning" course at Yonsei University._

### __Overview__

This project examines how mine tailings, the residual mineral waste left after ore processing, cause long-term environmental pollution in abandoned metal mines across Korea. Focusing on Korean cases, our team analyzed the generation of acid mine drainage (AMD), heavy-metal contamination pathways, human health impacts, and the national system responsible for mine reclamation. Based on this analysis, we proposed improved mitigation and restoration strategies tailored to the specific environmental and institutional context of Korea.
<br>

### __Background__

__Mine Tailings as a Source of Pollution__

Tailings contain sulfide minerals and heavy metals. When exposed to oxygen or water after mining operations cease, they oxidize and form acid mine drainage. This highly acidic solution dissolves metals such as cadmium, arsenic, and lead, spreading contamination.

According to our research, this process leads to:
- Formation of acidic leachate
- Mobilization of heavy-metal ions
- Contamination of groundwater and downstream rivers
- Long-term ecological and human health risks
<br>

__Human Health Impacts__

The project categorized toxic effects into:
- **Cadmium:** Kidney dysfunction, bone disease, and carcinogenicity
- **Arsenic:** Skin lesions, vascular disorders, and neurological impacts
- **Lead:** Developmental disorders and cardiovascular impacts

These risks are amplified when contaminated water or soil accumulates over long periods near residential areas.
<br>

### __Process__

__1. Review of Korea’s Abandoned Mine Reclamation Framework__

The Korean system operates through a sequence of planning stages: a Mine Status Survey, followed by a 5-Year Basic Mine Reclamation Plan, and finally an Annual Implementation Plan. The national target is to complete restoration for 30% of abandoned mines by 2026. However, actual progress has been slow due to limited budgets and institutional bottlenecks.

__2. Analysis of the 2023 Korea Mine Reclamation Plan__

We reviewed the 2023 annual plan to understand funding structures, project types, remediation priorities, and site selection criteria. The plan highlights that AMD treatment, contaminated soil removal, and stabilization works remain the core components of Korean mine restoration.

__3. Comparison with U.S. Case: Pennsylvania__

To evaluate institutional gaps, we compared Korea with Pennsylvania, a state known for its active abandoned mine reclamation programs.
* **Pennsylvania:** Multiple actors, including volunteer groups, local governments, and associations, collaborate to address tailings and AMD.
* **Korea:** KOMIR acts as the single major actor, resulting in limited flexibility and slower response times.
<br>

### __Analysis__

__Environmental Pathways of Tailings Pollution__

The analysis identified three main pathways:
1.  Sulfide oxidation leading to acidic water generation
2.  Heavy-metal dissolution and groundwater contamination
3.  Downstream pollution through runoff and seepage

__Limitations of Korean Remediation Policy__

We identified systemic issues reducing the effectiveness of current policies:
1.  **Delayed Remediation:** Pollution typically begins immediately after mine abandonment, yet the official remediation process often lags significantly.
2.  **Weak Accountability:** When mine owners dissolve or vanish, there is often no responsible entity to fund cleanup, leaving the burden on government budgets.
3.  **Fragmented Procedures:** The restoration process differs between operating mines and abandoned mines, causing bureaucratic delays.
4.  **Overreliance on the Public Sector:** Unlike the U.S., where diverse actors support reclamation financially and operationally, Korea’s reliance on a single agency limits overall capacity.
<br>

### __Proposed Improvement Strategies__

#### __1. Technical Approaches for Mitigating Pollution__

**Mitigation Techniques**
* Neutralizing acidic drainage through ADA tests and geochemical evaluation
* Removing or stabilizing tailings containing heavy metals
* Using passive limestone drains for continuous treatment

**Adaptation Techniques**
* Soil washing and flushing where metal concentrations are high
* Physically separating contaminated layers
* Vegetation-based stabilization

#### __2. Strengthening the Institutional System__

Based on our findings, we proposed the following policy changes:
* Require mandatory submission of "post-closure impact plans" during mine shutdown procedures.
* Create annual reclamation funds financed partly by mining operators.
* Increase investment in field monitoring and safety management.
* Diversify stakeholders beyond KOMIR, adopting a collaborative model similar to that of the U.S.
<br>

### __Results__

__Final Output__

* A structured analysis of contamination induced by mine tailings in Korea
* A review of national and international restoration frameworks
* A technical comparison of remediation methods
* Policy proposals tailored to Korea’s environmental and institutional context

__Significance__

This project demonstrates how environmental planning can integrate geochemical processes, health risk assessment, and policy analysis to address persistent contamination issues caused by mine tailings. It highlights the urgency of proactive remediation in Korea and the necessity for a more resilient, collaborative national framework.
