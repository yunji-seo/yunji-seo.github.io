### __Overview__

In modern urban planning, "Job-Housing Proximity" (putting homes near workplaces) is often praised for reducing commuting costs and energy consumption. However, when the workplace is an **Industrial Complex**, this proximity raises serious livability concerns. This report investigates the health risks and odor issues associated with apartments built near major industrial sites in Korea (e.g., Ulsan, Pohang) and proposes alternative planning strategies.
<br>

### __Background__

Regional cities have actively developed apartments near industrial complexes to create jobs and revitalize local economies. While these developments are marketed as convenient "work-life balance" solutions, they often overlook the unique environmental hazards of industrial zones compared to standard office districts. The report questions whether the convenience of a short commute justifies the potential compromise in environmental safety.
<br>

### __Key Analysis__

__1. Health Impacts of Invisible Pollutants__

Analysis of government health impact assessments revealed concerning patterns:
- **Findings:** Studies in areas like Ulsan and Siwha/Banwol showed higher concentrations of VOCs and heavy metals near residential zones compared to control groups.
- **The "Causality" Dilemma:** While correlations with respiratory and cardiovascular diseases were found, proving a direct causal link remains statistically difficult due to study limitations. However, the report argues that the *potential* long-term risk to residents, especially the elderly, is a significant threat to livability that cannot be ignored.

__2. The Odor Discrepancy__

One of the most immediate impacts on quality of life is odor.
- **Sensory vs. Technical:** There is a significant gap between technical measurements (which often meet legal standards) and the actual sensory experience of residents.
- **Nighttime Issues:** Odor complaints frequently spike at night or shift with wind direction, times when official inspections are less frequent or effective. This creates a situation where the environment is "legally safe" but "residentially uninhabitable."
<br>

### __Proposed Solutions__

The report concludes that the concept of "Job-Housing Proximity" should not be applied blindly to industrial zones.

__1. Policy Reform: Resident-Centric Standards__
Environmental regulations must be tightened to reflect resident perception (sensory impact) rather than just chemical concentration limits. This includes mandatory odor ordinances for industrial zones.

__2. Alternative Planning: Safe Distance + Connectivity__
Instead of placing housing immediately next to factories, planning should prioritize **"Buffer Zones."**
- The report suggests maintaining a safe physical distance to mitigate pollution risks.
- To compensate for the distance, **transport connectivity** should be improved. This model solves the commuting issue through efficient transit rather than physical proximity, ensuring both convenience and health.
