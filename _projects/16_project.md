_This project was completed as part of "Writing as a Cultural Critique" course at Yonsei University._

### __Overview__

"It takes a village to raise a child." This proverb highlights the communal responsibility in child-rearing. However, modern Korean society is witnessing a rise in gated communities where residents explicitly ban non-resident children from using complex playgrounds. This essay critiques this phenomenon not merely as a property dispute, but as a sociological issue of **exclusion and dehumanization**, applying Giorgio Agamben’s *Remnants of Auschwitz* and Victor Turner’s concept of *Liminality*.
<br>

### __Social Phenomenon: The Gated Playground__

While apartment playgrounds are legally classified as welfare facilities for residents, they have traditionally served as open communal spaces for neighborhood children. Recently, however, fences and "Residents Only" signs have proliferated, physically barring "outsider" children.
* **Legal Ambiguity:** While residents argue for property rights, housing laws suggest these facilities should be open to the neighboring community.
* **The Core Issue:** Beyond legality, this represents a severe ethical breach in how society treats children and defines community boundaries.
<br>

### __Theoretical Analysis__

__1. Humanity and Communication (Giorgio Agamben)__
Drawing from Agamben’s *Remnants of Auschwitz*, the essay argues that humanity is defined not just by biological existence (*zoe*), but by political existence and communication (*bios*).
* **Communication as Humanity:** Humans become social beings through interaction with the "other."
* **The Danger of Exclusion:** By building physical barriers (fences) around playgrounds, residents cut off the possibility of communication. This act of exclusion essentially denies the "outsider" children the opportunity to interact and form their identities, subtly dehumanizing them in the eyes of the community.

__2. Playgrounds as Liminal Spaces (Victor Turner)__
Using Turner’s anthropological theory, playgrounds are defined as **"Liminal Spaces"** (thresholds).
* **Freedom from Hierarchy:** Play spaces should be free from existing social orders and hierarchies, allowing children to experiment with new roles and relationships.
* **Imposing Class Structure:** When adults impose capitalist logic (property value, management fees) onto these spaces, they destroy the liminality. The playground ceases to be a space of freedom and becomes a tool for reinforcing class distinction and parental desire for status reproduction.
<br>

### __Conclusion__

The exclusion of children from playgrounds is a manifestation of distorted parental desires to inherit social status and segregate based on economic class. The essay concludes that the right to play is fundamental to a child's identity formation.

To build a healthy future society, we must move beyond the logic of private ownership and restore the playground as an inclusive space. As the proverb says, raising a child truly requires the openness and care of the entire village, not just a gated complex.
